> I am designing a **5-day college canteen operation plan** for a project. The total available operating/procurement budget is **₹10,000 for one week**.
>
> I need you to create a **complete, numerical, internally consistent canteen plan** covering:
>
> **food selection → pricing → production cost → expected demand → quantity to stock → daily inventory → attendance variation → peak lunch demand → whole-day snacks → beverages → packaged products → wastage control → emergency reserve → revenue → profit/surplus.**
>
> Do not give me a generic answer. Perform the calculations and produce a practical plan that a college canteen manager could actually implement.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. BASIC OPERATING CONDITIONS
>
> Assume:
>
> - The canteen operates for **5 college days per week**.
> - Total weekly budget = **₹10,000**.
> - This ₹10,000 is the maximum amount available for food procurement/production, packaged products, beverages, basic disposables, and emergency replenishment.
> - Keep approximately **10% of the total budget as an emergency reserve**, unless your calculations demonstrate a better allocation.
> - The student population is larger than the number we can serve under the ₹10,000 pilot budget, so treat this as a **controlled pilot inventory system**, not an assumption that the entire college will be served.
> - All assumptions about student footfall, number of customers, and demand must be clearly stated before calculations.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. DEMAND PATTERN
>
> Demand is **not constant**.
>
> Model demand using both **time of day** and **day-to-day attendance variation**.
>
> ### Morning
>
> Morning demand is expected to be high for:
>
> - Tea
> - Coffee
> - Light breakfast
> - Quick snacks
>
> Students generally prefer quick and affordable items before or between classes.
>
> ### Lunch
>
> Lunch is the **major peak-demand period**.
>
> During lunch:
>
> - Demand for fresh lunch meals rises sharply.
> - Demand for water rises.
> - Demand for juices and canned/packaged beverages rises.
> - Some students will still purchase snacks.
> - The canteen must avoid running out of popular products.
>
> ### Post-lunch
>
> Demand is expected to decline compared with the lunch rush.
>
> However:
>
> - Snacks should remain available.
> - Packaged snacks should remain available.
> - Water, juices, cans, and other beverages should remain available.
> - Tea/coffee can continue to be sold.
>
> ### Day-to-day attendance
>
> Assume the week contains:
>
> - **Normal-attendance days**
> - **High-attendance days**
> - **At least one low-attendance day**
>
> Demand must therefore change from day to day.
>
> Do not use identical production quantities for all five days.
>
> ---
>
> # 3. MENU DESIGN
>
> Build a **limited, practical menu** rather than an unnecessarily large menu.
>
> Divide the menu into:
>
> ### A. Morning items
>
> Include:
>
> - Tea
> - Coffee
> - Poha
> - Idli-Sambar
> - Veg Sandwich
>
> ### B. Whole-day fresh snacks
>
> Snacks must be available **throughout the day**, not only in the morning or lunch period.
>
> Consider:
>
> - Samosa
> - Veg Roll
> - Veg Momos
>
> Fresh snacks should be replenished in **small batches**, rather than preparing the entire day's quantity at once.
>
> ### C. Whole-day packaged snacks
>
> Include appropriate packaged options such as:
>
> - Chips
> - Biscuits
> - Namkeen/other low-cost packaged snacks
>
> Consider their:
>
> - Shelf life
> - Storage
> - Purchase cost
> - Demand
> - Stock-out risk
>
> ### D. Lunch meals
>
> Main lunch meals must be priced within approximately **₹150–₹200**.
>
> Use a practical menu such as:
>
> - Rajma Rice
> - Chole Rice
> - Veg Pulao
> - Veg Noodles
> - Paneer Rice
> - Special Veg Meal
> - Premium Veg Thali
>
> Do not necessarily offer every lunch item every day.
>
> Use **daily rotation** so that ingredient variety and wastage remain under control.
>
> ### E. Whole-day beverages
>
> Include:
>
> - 500 ml packaged water
> - 1 L packaged water where practical
> - Packaged fruit juice
> - Juice box
> - Soft-drink cans
> - Tea
> - Coffee
>
> Beverage demand should increase during the lunch rush.
>
> ---
>
> # 4. PRICING RULE
>
> Main meals must be approximately **₹150–₹200**.
>
> The goal is that a student's normal lunch/food expenditure can remain around the **₹150–₹200 range**.
>
> Do **not** interpret this as meaning that snacks, beverages, or packaged products must individually cost ₹150–₹200.
>
> Set reasonable lower prices for:
>
> - Tea
> - Coffee
> - Snacks
> - Water
> - Juice
> - Chips
> - Biscuits

>
> **Selling price | Estimated procurement/production cost | Profit contribution per unit**
>
> Clearly distinguish:
>
> **selling price** from **cost price**.
>
